What it is

Opti looks at a small business the way a consultant would: what is working, what isn't and why. The page gives the method away. Each post teaches one check an owner can run on their own numbers, and it never sells.

Who it's for

Owners of small shops, cafes and service businesses who don't have a finance or marketing team. The examples use riyals and everyday situations, like a shop that sells four units a day.

What I do

I pick the topics, find and read the primary sources, write the posts and the longer research articles, and schedule everything across the three platforms.

The system

A small page needs a structure it can repeat. Every topic sits under one of six levers, every post belongs to a series, and every post follows the same four rules.

Four rules
  • Facts come from the original source, named on the slide.
  • If a source gives a range, the post shows the range.
  • Examples and opinions are labelled.
  • Results are never invented.

The Six Levers: Pricing

1% on price beats 1% more customers

A McKinsey analysis of 2,463 companies, published in Harvard Business Review in 1992, found that a 1% better price raised operating profit by 11.1%, against 3.3% for 1% more volume. The carousel then works it through on a small shop's numbers in riyals.

Check your business

When should you reorder stock?

Reorder point is average daily sales times supplier lead time, plus safety stock. The post gives the formula, a worked example for a shop selling four units a day and three questions to ask yourself.

Tip of the day

Before Instagram: finish your Google profile

Google says customers are 70% more likely to visit a business with a complete Business Profile. The post lists three fixes that take about an hour.

The Six Levers: Cash and stock

How many days could you last with no sales?

JPMorgan Chase Institute tracked 597,000 US small businesses and found the median held 27 days of cash. The post shows how to find your own number from a bank statement, and says plainly that no comparable Saudi figure was found.

The Six Levers: Operations

26 basic practices. The average firm does 39%.

McKenzie and Woodruff scored more than 20,000 small firms in seven countries on 26 basic practices. Firms that did more of them were more productive, and the post is careful to call that a correlation, not proof.
